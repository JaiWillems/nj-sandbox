# Overview

The Spyder drone is an interest project to explore mechatronic concepts applied to aerial systems. The goal was to develop a quadrotor capapable of controlled flight with extensibility for capable payloads including autonomous operations. To align with this learning objective, the platform was developed from the ground up with a fully custom airframe, bespoke controller design and implementation, and arduino class hardware. The result is a crude implementation with personality, quirks, and bruises.

## Quadrotor Dynamics (Plant)

The conventions used to describe the quadrotor dynamics are defined in [Figure 6](#fig-definition). Angular displacements about the x, y, and z axes are captured by the roll, pitch, and yaw angles, respectively, where positive displacement is governed by the right hand rule. Each motor, labelled from one through four, impose a force (F) and reactive torque (M) on the drone, whose imbalances result in acceleration. Motors one and three rotate counter-clockwise, as seen from above, while motors two and four rotate clockwise.

<a id="fig-definition"></a>
<div align="center">
  <img src="/design/Photos/Conventions.png" alt="Quadrotor Coordinate Frame and Conventions">
  <br>
  <em>Figure 6: Quadrotor Coordinate Frame and Conventions</em>
</div>

The forces and torques generated by the motors combine to form the control signals (U) as defined below where $P_i\in[0, 100]$ for $i=\{1, 2, 3, 4\}$ are the individual motor Pulse Width Modulation (PWM) signals, $k_F$ is the slope of the motor's thrust to PWM curve, and $k_M$ is the slope of the motor torque to PWM curve.

$$
\begin{aligned}
U_1&=F_1+F_2+F_3+F_4=k_F(P_1+P_2+P_3+P_4) & (\text{Altitude})\\
U_2&=\frac{L\sqrt{2}}{2}(F_3+F_4-F_1-F_2)=\frac{Lk_F\sqrt{2}}{2}(P_3+P_4-P_1-P_2) & (\text{Roll})\\
U_3&=\frac{L\sqrt{2}}{2}(F_3+F_2-F_1-F_4)=\frac{Lk_F\sqrt{2}}{2}(P_3+P_2-P_1-P_4) & (\text{Pitch})\\
U_4&=M_2-M_1+M_4-M_3=k_M(P_2-P_1+P_4-P_3) & (\text{Yaw})
\end{aligned}
$$

Taken from Heja Cengiz's work on [Quadcopter Modeling and LQR Design](https://uu.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:1870673/FULLTEXT01.pdf), the governing equiations of the quadrotor dynamics are then given by the following system of equations,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\dot{x}&=uc(\psi)c(\theta)+v[c(\psi)s(\theta)s(\phi)-c(\phi)s(\psi)]+w[c(\psi)c(\phi)s(\theta)+s(\psi)s(\phi)]\\
\dot{y}&=us(\psi)c(\theta)+v[s(\psi)s(\theta)s(\phi)+c(\phi)c(\psi)]+w[s(\psi)c(\phi)s(\theta)-c(\psi)s(\phi)]\\
\dot{z}&=-us(\theta)+vc(\theta)s(\phi)+wc(\theta)c(\phi)\\
\dot{\phi}&=p+qs(\phi)t(\theta)+rc(\phi)t(\theta)\\
\dot{\theta}&=qc(\phi)-rs(\phi)\\
\dot{\psi}&=qs(\phi)sec(\theta)+rc(\phi)sec(\theta)\\
\dot{u}&=gs(\theta)-qw+rv\\
\dot{v}&=-gc(\theta)s(\phi)-ru+pw\\
\dot{w}&=-gc(\theta)c(\phi)+\frac{U_1}{m}-pv+qu\\
\dot{p}&=\frac{U_2}{I_x}+qr\frac{I_y-I_z}{I_x}\\
\dot{q}&=\frac{U_3}{I_y}+rp\frac{I_z-I_x}{I_y}\\
\dot{r}&=\frac{U_4}{I_z}+pq\frac{I_x-I_y}{I_z}\\
\end{aligned}
$$

where $[x,y,z]$ and $[\phi,\theta,\psi]$ are linear and angular positions in the Earth reference frame, respectively, while $[p,q,r]$ and $[u,v,w]$ are linear and angular velocities in the body reference frame, respectively. The presented dynamics model does not address affects due to air resistance.

The Spyder quadrotor has the parameter values as given in the following table.

| Desctiption | Parameter | Value [Unit] |
| :---------- | :-------: | :----------: |
| Mass | $m$ | 1.04100 [kg] |

## Controller Architecture

As seen in [Figure 7](#fig-control-architecture), the controller design uses four PID loops to track altitude rate, roll angle, pitch angle, and yaw rate. The PID loops consume error signals computed from reference and state data, and return the control signals ($U_i$ for $i=1,2,3,4$). Preliminary tuning of controller gains were performed using MATLAB's Simulink environment, discussed more in [Simulink Implementation and Results](#simulink-implementation-and-results).

## Simulink Implementation and Results

A MATLAB Simulink model was implemented to assist in tuning PID gains and assessing the performance of the control architecture. The high-level control loop is shown in [Figure 8](#fig-simulink-control-loop). The Ground Station block receives digital signals from ground station hardware to support hardware-in-the-loop simulation, and performs signal calibration and mapping, as discussed in [Ground Station Calibration and Signal Mapping](#ground-station-calibration-and-signal-mapping). The Controller block implements the PID controllers as demonstrated in [Controller Architecture](#controller-architecture). Lastly, the Drone Model block implements the physics, detailed in [Quadrotor Dynamics (Plant)](#quadrotor-dynamics-plant), in addition to enforcing smiple hardware limitations. A complete export of the Simulink model is provided in pdf form [here](/design/Photos/Simulink/Complete%20Simulink%20Model.pdf).
